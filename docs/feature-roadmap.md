# VoltRoute feature order

User-approved order, updated September 15, 2026. This replaces the earlier 16-feature backlog. Features are delivered incrementally in this order.

## Phase 1: Core foundation

1. **Data Freshness: implemented.** Source, observation age and directory retrieval are separate. Live requires a valid operator observation up to 5 minutes old. Operator observations up to 30 minutes and personal reports up to 24 hours are Recent. Missing, invalid and expired observations are Unknown. Badges age every 30 seconds and on tab focus. There is currently no operator feed; ordinary directory records therefore remain Unknown. Personal reports are private, not community aggregates.
2. **Price Confidence: implemented.** Verified requires an applicable USD/kWh operator tariff with a station match, source URL, an observation within 24 hours and an unexpired validity period. No operator tariffs are currently connected. A single explicit community rate or user-entered rate is Estimated. Missing, ambiguous and unsupported tariffs remain Unavailable, with no default rate. Manual rates belong to the selected station and browser session. Charging cost remains an estimate even when the rate is verified.
3. **Smart Stop: implemented for the next stop and conditional one-stop trips.** Search a 10-mile corridor along the reachable first part of the road route. Shortlist up to 16 of 400 mapped records, check directed road distances and travel times, and reject stops that fail reserve, detour, mapped access, known failure or arrival-hours checks. Select by extra driving plus explicit adjustments for early charging, connector-specific speed, availability, hours and access, or the selected preference below. Recheck finalists against full road routes before returning a winner. Optional user-entered battery capacity and vehicle power enable rough charge-time estimates. A single planned charge may cover the destination with reserve; label that successful-charge assumption. Routes requiring additional charging stops remain incomplete. Operation, network/adapter access and incomplete data coverage remain explicit.
4. **Why This Charger?: implemented.** Show arrival battery, road distance, extra driving, connector-specific power, arrival hours and evidence behind the choice, along with the ranking adjustments and limitations. Suggestions expire after 10 minutes or when their supporting observation changes freshness category. Editing trip inputs, charging constraints or personal reports clears the old suggestion.
5. **Backup Charger: implemented for the recommended next stop.** Use directed station-to-station road connections to find a matching-connector backup at a separate site (at least 0.25 miles away, different record and no matching listed address). Calculate backup arrival battery from start → main → backup with **no energy gained at the main stop**, then deduct an explicit failure allowance (default 3 percentage points) while retaining the user's reserve. Include a failure delay (default 10 minutes) in station-local arrival-hours checks. Reject known busy/broken/planned stations, restricted access, bad road snaps and insufficient battery. Recheck the chosen backup against its full road geometry. Display main → backup distance, time, remaining battery, hours, access, evidence, same-network risk and a dashed map route; backup food/restroom details use the existing nearby search. No operation or free-port guarantee is implied.
6. **No-Stranding Mode: implemented as a next-stop planning constraint.** On by default. A recommendation requires a separate reachable backup with listed public access and opening hours covering estimated arrival. Missing hours/access cannot qualify. If no backup passes, withhold the recommendation; never silently turn the mode off. Users can explicitly turn it off to see provisional or unbacked main stops. Full-road revalidation may try two main candidates and two backups per main; failed checks cannot leave an old winner selected. Suggestions and map paths expire when main or backup evidence ages, arrival hours close, inputs/reports change, or ten minutes elapse. This checks one failure scenario, not the rest of the trip, severe weather losses, future port availability or physical safety.
7. **Trip Safety Score: implemented as a planning-confidence checklist.** Show an explicit 0–100 score with earned/possible points and reasons. Weights: charging-plan mileage coverage 40, battery above reserve 25, current observations 15, public access/arrival hours 10, independent backup 10. Charger-only checks are not applicable for a route needing no charge. Use the weaker main/backup observation: live available gets full credit, recent working/available 40%, missing evidence none. No backup halves the main observation credit. A listed open status, fresh directory fetch, power or price cannot create availability credit. Apply visible limits: 85 for unmodeled driving conditions and manually entered range, 59 for an incomplete itinerary, 39 for missing backup/reserve support, and 24 when charging is needed but no main stop is recommended. The score measures support for the whole requested route, including the unassessed remainder; it is not an empirically calibrated failure probability or physical-safety rating. Invalid or expired plans have no score.
8. **Route Risk Map: implemented.** Color the actual returned road geometry with a labeled key and an accessible section list. Green requires the relevant current checks plus at least 10 battery percentage points above reserve; charging-dependent green also requires current available-port observations and access/hours/backup checks. Yellow marks missing evidence or a narrowing buffer. Red marks a reserve shortfall without an intervening planned charge, not proof that no chargers exist. If one charge can cover the remaining road with reserve, project the final leg with an explicit successful-charge assumption and include destination reserve in the score. Otherwise, later miles remain grey and unassessed. The dashed backup branch always deducts the failure allowance and assumes no charge at the main. Users can toggle colors, focus sections, inspect reasons/assumptions/battery estimates, and return to the full route. Preserve road vertices and interpolate only section boundaries, which are approximate. No new routing requests or paid services are required for these views.

## Phase 2: User value

9. **Fastest / Cheapest / Safest / Balanced: implemented with explicit comparison scope.** Balanced preserves the existing next-stop ranking. Safest prioritizes qualifying backup support, current observations, listed access/hours, then the weaker main/backup battery arrival, not physical-safety certification. Fastest compares driving plus estimated charging time for supported one-stop trips; it requires battery capacity and connector/vehicle power. Cheapest compares charging energy required to reach the same destination with reserve, multiplied by a supported station-specific rate; it requires battery capacity and price. It does not rank by rate alone. Unpriced, incomplete and missing-time options cannot win the relevant comparison, and the mode never silently switches. Revalidate up to two finalists and their backup routes before the final comparison; this is not a global optimum over every possible route. Show evaluated/comparable counts and overlapping data gaps. Manual rates are bounded, station-specific, validated and account-private in the request, never written to the shared provider cache. Editing rates clears the old plan; a Cheapest plan expires if its applicable tariff changes confidence, source or amount.
10. **Total Trip Cost: implemented as an in-trip charging-energy budget.** A route needing no charge shows zero additional charging purchases, excluding starting battery energy. A complete conditional one-stop budget uses entered usable battery capacity × battery percentage added × supported rate; operator tariffs must remain applicable through arrival and the estimated session when its duration is known. Report each rate's confidence and source. Missing capacity or rate leaves the cost unavailable, with no defaults. If more charging stops are required, show only the known next-stop subtotal and keep the trip total unavailable, even if that known subtotal is zero. Do not double-count the backup as a second normal session. Charging losses, taxes, tolls, parking, session and idle fees are excluded explicitly. Complete multi-stop budgets remain dependent on a future multi-stop itinerary; no prices for unplanned stops are invented.
11. **Charging Break Planner: implemented with explicit walking assumptions.** Find food, coffee, explicitly mapped restrooms and selected shopping types within half a mile. Use the selected main stop’s current charge-time estimate or a user-entered break window. Activity duration is editable. Compare round-trip straight-line walking at an assumed 2.5 mph plus a five-minute buffer and the visit allowance. Check listed local schedules throughout the visit, including intermediate closures. Unknown hours/access, customer restrictions and stale listings require review; actual walking paths, crossings, queues and opening exceptions are unverified. Evaluate at most 40 nearby places per activity and display up to eight. Shopping does not imply a restroom.
12. **Route Comparison: implemented for up to two checked charging-stop routes.** Retain full road geometry and backup validation for the top two eligible main stops, including Balanced mode. Show driving, estimated charging, supported trip-energy cost and planning confidence. Longer incomplete charging itineraries show a next-stop subtotal and no complete trip time/cost. Unknown inputs stay unavailable. Selecting a route updates the main, backup, geometry and tariff snapshot together without renewing the calculation expiry. Reject options with expired evidence or failed backup requirements. If the second lookup fails, preserve a completed first option and explain the limited comparison; never substitute an unchecked candidate. This is a bounded comparison via different chargers, not all road alternatives or a complete multi-stop itinerary.
13. **Charger History: implemented as timestamped observation history.** Append private working/busy/broken reports transactionally alongside the current private report. Migrate only the last saved report, preserving its timestamp; overwritten past reports cannot be recovered. Keep the newest 500 private entries per account across stations and show up to 100 per station. A dedicated control deletes that station’s private history and current report. Community history shows up to 100 entries from the last 30 days with counts of self-reported successful charging, busy and problem observations. These counts are not reliability percentages, utilization, or busy-time forecasts.
14. **Community Verification: implemented as consented community confirmations, not independent verification.** Separate structured successful-charge/busy/problem observations require explicit sharing consent and attestation of a personal observation now. Timestamps and owner identity are server assigned. One entry per driver/station/UTC day limits duplicate counts; sharing again updates that entry. Other viewers never receive names, emails, notes or author IDs. Withdrawal is owner-scoped. Other authenticated viewers can flag an inaccurate observation, which hides it automatically; this is not human moderation. A flagged daily entry cannot be restored through withdrawal/reposting. Community observations do not become operator evidence or live availability. Existing Site access is preserved, currently owner-only, so a wider community requires a separately authorized audience change.

## Phase 3: Advanced intelligence

15. **Expected Wait: manual queue allowance implemented; live arrival-time prediction pending operator data.** Require entered vehicles ahead, usable matching ports, occupied matching ports and assumed session duration. Use currently free slots first, then equal-duration charging waves under explicit FIFO/no-new-arrivals/full-session-remaining assumptions. Reject inconsistent counts; expire the observation after five minutes and clear it on edits or station/connector changes. Never derive queues from community report counts or directory port totals. Live wait prediction remains explicitly unavailable.
16. **Charge Session Assistant: manual tracking implemented.** Require entered usable capacity, starting/target battery and expected average charging power. Keep a session tied to its original charger/vehicle when browsing other stations. Calculate target energy, estimated finish time and an at-start energy budget using only supported station rates; operator prices must remain valid through the predicted finish. Remove the old range-inferred capacity calculator from the UI. Use absolute timestamps so in-page reminders recover on focus after a suspended tab. Estimated completion prompts a vehicle check but never claims measured battery or starts idle charges. Only manually marking charging finished starts an entered grace-period reminder; missing fee/timing remains unavailable. Idle allowance uses the entered rate and elapsed time, not an operator invoice. No charger control, telemetry, background push, payment or automatic community reporting. Reloading ends tracking, disclosed in the UI.
17. **Trip Rescue: implemented with manual current inputs.** Recalculate from updated location and battery, exclude the failed charger and retain road, reserve and backup checks.
18. **Road Trip Watch: page-open rechecks implemented; live monitoring pending feeds.** Two-minute checks pause on stale current inputs or errors. Directory refresh is explicitly not live availability monitoring.

## Additional hours update included

- Open Now and 24/7 filters evaluate supported weekly schedules in station local time, including overnight hours and daylight-saving transitions.
- Conditional, malformed and missing schedules remain unconfirmed and are excluded when those filters are on.
- Time-zone lookup from coordinates is marked estimated. Detected boundary ambiguity remains unknown.
- Opening schedules never imply working chargers or free charging ports.

## Routing contracts

- [OSRM HTTP API](https://project-osrm.org/docs/v5.24.0/api/): directed route/table distances, durations, geometry and snapped waypoints. No straight-line fallback distances are requested or accepted.
- [Overpass query language](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Overpass_API/Overpass_QL): retrieve mapped chargers inside a bounding box around the reachable route, then shortlist within approximately 10 miles of sampled route points. Results are limited and community coverage is not guaranteed.
- Road and directory responses may be cached for one hour. A route calculation is not a live traffic or charger-status observation. Rankings and personal reports are never stored in the shared provider cache.

## Directory outage recovery

- Replace the single failing Overpass endpoint with two independent, global OSM services: Private.coffee, then VK Maps on a transient failure. Both are listed in the [OSM public-instance directory](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Overpass_API#Public_Overpass_API_instances); Private.coffee also lists [its Overpass service](https://private.coffee/services.html). `OVERPASS_URL` and optional `OVERPASS_FALLBACK_URL` can supply managed operator endpoints. Do not use the old overloaded main instance by default.
- Use sequential POST queries with a 24-second timeout per connection, at most two attempts. HTTP 5xx, transport errors and malformed/incomplete responses can trigger fallback. Genuine empty results are accepted. Quota/access/request rejections do not trigger provider rotation. App rate-limit waits are separate from upstream errors.
- Explore stations and food/restrooms keep successful responses for one hour. If a refresh fails, a same-query snapshot retrieved within 24 hours can be displayed with an explicit warning and its original retrieval timestamp. Never replace failed responses with an empty array, extend a stale snapshot's timestamp, or count directory retrieval as live charger evidence. Smart Stop does not use this stale fallback.
- Failed loading displays “Charger search unavailable” instead of “0 mapped charging stations.” Retry keeps the searched location and radius. Superseded browser requests are aborted. The error offers an optional Google Maps search for the same center. Failed amenities display unknown counts.


## Trip Rescue and Road Trip Watch

17. Trip Rescue implemented: explicitly updated location/battery, optional failed-main exclusion, same reserve/No-Stranding and price preferences, existing directed road checks, reviewed directions. Excluded stations are removed before main and backup evaluation. No roadside dispatch or guaranteed reachability. Limited corridor coverage still applies.
18. Road Trip Watch implemented as visible-page rechecks every two minutes with one request at a time. Position/battery expire after five minutes; input changes cancel checks. Provider errors stop automatic checks. No live operator feed or vehicle telemetry; directory cache can be up to one hour old. Results are separate from the original map and require driver review. Reload ends tracking. Full continuous live monitoring awaits operator integrations.

Validation: trip-watch tests cover invalid current inputs, reserve/backup preservation, failed-station filtering, position expiry and hidden/overlapping polling guards.


## Driver video review

User recordings showed the completed Smart Stop, route comparison, backup and nearby-place sections. They also showed the center column becoming empty while scrolling long side panels. Keep the center map/results pane visible on three-column desktop layouts, with a bounded independently scrollable pane and keyboard focus. Place the map before the detailed assessment/budget and the selected stop before route comparison and recovery tools. Comparison remains available in a closed-by-default disclosure. Tablet/mobile retain normal page scrolling. Live routing and mobile browser QA are not independently verified by this layout change.

## Map and mobile usability
- Neutral numbered groups cluster nearby mapped stations at the current zoom. Selected, main and backup stations stay individual. Groups zoom on activation; at maximum zoom a scrollable station chooser handles overlapping coordinates. Counts do not imply working or available ports.
- Tablet/mobile section shortcuts reach search, map and charger details. Selected-station details link includes food/restrooms. Phone station results use normal page scrolling and controls have larger touch targets.
- Type checking and production build checked. Signed-in mobile browser QA remains unverified because the supervised preview sign-in route previously returned 404; authentication was not changed.

## Clearer missing-data guidance
- Unconfirmed charger status explains that unknown does not mean broken and directs drivers to check the network app. Source dates are available in expandable details.
- Opening hours link directly to the original station record, with confirmation guidance for unknown schedules.
- Community free listings say “Listed as free” rather than presenting $0/kWh as a current tariff. Detail text explains that estimates still use the listed zero energy rate, with other fees unconfirmed. Pricing, freshness, and routing rules are unchanged.

## First-use guide
- Collapsible four-step guide links to vehicle selection, Explore, Plan a trip, and charger details. Search and trip links activate the matching tab without changing entered values or running a search.
- Explains 100–250-mile search areas versus the 250-result cap, connector checks, backup review and missing live-port data. No additional data collection or saved onboarding state.
- Type check and production build validation; signed-in browser interaction remains unverified due the preview sign-in limitation.
